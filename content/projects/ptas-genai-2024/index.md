Generative AI arrived in university computer science teaching faster than policy or educational wisdom. Staff and students are both using it, often without a shared understanding of what counts as appropriate use, and there was little evidence about what people were actually doing with it.

This £17,563 Principal's Teaching Award Scheme project at the University of Edinburgh set out to gather that evidence. I worked on it as a Co-Investigator, alongside colleagues in Informatics, Mathematics, and Business -- a deliberately cross-School team, since the questions are not specific to any one discipline.

The project ran surveys and focus groups with staff and students to understand current and potential use cases for generative AI in computer science education at the university, with the aim of informing how the institution shapes its integration rather than simply reacting to it.

The Principal Investigator, Pavlos Andreadis, summarises the findings and recommendations on [his project page](https://cortu01.com/genai.html).

We presented ["Perspectives from a PTAS project on understanding and shaping generative AI integration in computer science education"](https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/perspectives-from-a-ptas-project-on-understanding-and-shaping-gen-2/) at the [University of Edinburgh Learning & Teaching Conference](https://registryservices.ed.ac.uk/academic-development/events/learning-teaching-conference/archive/2025) on 17 June 2025.
