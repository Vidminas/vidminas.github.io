CHAILD is a UKRI-funded project investigating children's and young people's perceptions, uses, needs, and challenges with generative AI. It is where I currently work as a Postdoctoral Research Fellow, in a joint post between the [Department of Computer Science at the University of Oxford](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/) and the [Institute of Education at University College London](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/ioe/), supervised by Dr Jun Zhao and Professor Manolis Mavrikis.

As generative AI and synthetic media applications improve, people use them for more and more tasks. There is a fine line between gaining efficiency by using an application as a tool and ceding control to it by having it do the task for you. That line is especially hard to navigate for children and young people, who are still developing their own decision-making abilities and are usually also constrained -- often for good reasons -- by parents, teachers, and policymakers.

The question we work on is how AI systems and applications might be designed to support children and young people in making informed decisions and enacting them meaningfully: their **agency**.

The project is interdisciplinary by design, bringing together computer science, education, philosophy, and law. Alongside empirical studies with children and young people, we build prototype applications, run workshops with the research community, and work with teachers and policymakers.

I also lead a special issue of the [International Journal of Child-Computer Interaction on "Agency in Child-AI Interaction"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/special-issue/331176/agency-in-child-ai-interaction), which grew out of the [CAMCAD workshop](https://oxfordhcc.github.io/CAMCAD/) our team ran at CHI 2026.
